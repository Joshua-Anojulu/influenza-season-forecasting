Forecasting Influenza Season Peaks:
what the data can and cannot support

A characterization study of peak timing and peak severity across 22 seasons of national ILI data. Honest floors, a strict leakage firewall, and where off-the-shelf models actually stand.

Joshua · advised by Dr. Subhro Mitra · 2026-07-10 · preliminary and descriptive

Motivation & data

Two season-level questions, one honest test bed

  • Peak timing: the MMWR week ILI activity peaks.
  • Peak severity: the % WEIGHTED ILI at that peak.
  • Framed within-season: stand at decision week W, use only data through W, forecast the rest.
  • Validation is leave-one-season-out; decision weeks W = 8, 12, 16.

Data-quality finding

A week-52 holiday reporting artifact moves the target

  • Year-end reporting inflates a single week-52 ILI point in several seasons.
  • On the raw series this false spike becomes the labeled peak week in 5 of 22 seasons.
  • Fix: define targets on a 3-week centered smoother; keep raw values and audit flags alongside.
  • Verified: no non-week-52 season shifts by more than 2 weeks, so the smoother corrects the artifact without inventing a new one.

Model comparison · timing

No model beats the timing floor

  • Prophet: logistic trend rises monotonically to the horizon, so the predicted peak lands at the season end. 0% within ±1 at every W.
  • ARIMA: blows up in log space and clips flat to the historical-max cap, producing no locatable peak week in 10/13/5 of the 19/17/12 forecasts (W=8/12/16).
  • Gaussian curve: yields a well-defined peak week in only 5 / 1 / 0 forecast seasons at W=8 / 12 / 16.
  • The one apparent exception, stated plainly. On the 7 of 12 seasons where ARIMA does locate a peak at W=16, its MAE is 3.00 wk against 6.00 for the running-max floor. But those 7 are selected by the model's own success: the 5 it drops are the severe seasons (mean peak ILI 5.75 vs 4.29 for the 7 it keeps). Against LOSO climatology on the same 7 seasons (3.29 wk) the edge is 0.29 wk at n=7, and only 1 of 7 lands within ±1.
  • Root cause is not the model class: at realistic lead times the peak has not happened yet, so nothing in the data locates it.
Firewall audited: no post-W data enters any fit; predicted peaks are read only from the forecast region (sw > W). No leak flagged. Timing metrics use non-plateau forecasts only; the selection effect is quantified in results/05_survivorship.md.
Model comparison · severity

A simple regression is the first model to beat a floor

  • Univariate regression (peak severity from cumulative-ILI-through-W): MAE 1.26 / 1.12 / 0.92 at W=8/12/16. Beats climatology at every W; beats or ties the running-max floor at W=8, 12.
  • Ridge + strain + vaccine coverage: no better than ILI alone. Those covariates carry no extra severity signal at this sample size.
  • Gaussian: amplitude pins to the cap and overshoots (MAE 2.3-2.6 at W=8, 12), worse than climatology. Its apparent recovery to 1.47 at W=16 is scored on only 5 of 19 seasons, the ones its curve fit did not pin to a bound, so it is not comparable.
Real-time-honest: ILINet is the one signal genuinely in hand at W. Strain (reporting lag) and vaccine coverage (revised survey) enter only the retrospective explanatory model. Reference series use all 19 modeled seasons at every W; the Gaussian is scored on its forecast subset (n=19/18/5). See figures/09_severity_by_W.png.
Primary affirmative finding · calibration

Prophet's 80% intervals are severely overconfident

  • Prophet's intervals sit pinned near the historical-max ceiling: median 80% interval at W=12 is [7.09, 7.54] ILI.
  • At W=12, 14 of 17 forecast seasons fall under that interval (2 above, 1 inside), a median 2.20 ILI points below its lower bound.
  • Empirical 80% coverage is 5.9-10.5% across W, about an order of magnitude too low.
  • Same upward-extrapolation pathology as the point forecasts, now in the uncertainty bands.
Coverage is granular at n=12-19 (1-2 seasons covered; one season ≈ 6 percentage points). An earlier unseeded run read 11.8% at W=12 because two seasons sit within 0.04 ILI of an interval edge; the fit is now seeded (seed=42) and 5.9% is reproducible. The order-of-magnitude undercoverage holds either way. Per-season split: results/05_survivorship.md.
What the data supports

Honest characterization, two affirmative findings

  • Severity is forecastable from early-season ILI intensity by a simple regression that beats the naive floor.
  • Off-the-shelf uncertainty is not trustworthy: Prophet's 80% intervals cover the truth 5.9-10.5% of the time.
  • Timing is near the noise floor at realistic lead times, and no model class here overcomes it.
  • Strain, vaccine coverage, and a curve model add nothing at this sample size and these lead times.

The binding constraint is not the model. It is that, standing at week W, the peak has not yet occurred and the sample is only 19 seasons.

Next steps

Where this goes

  • Report calibration as a first-class metric, not point error alone; recalibrate or widen intervals honestly.
  • Test whether a mechanistic / compartmental curve (not a symmetric Gaussian) locates the turnover any earlier.
  • Robustness check on one or two HHS regions once the national methodology is locked.
  • Reframe the template's "±1 week on ≥70% of seasons" as an aspirational goal, not a deliverable, given the floor.
